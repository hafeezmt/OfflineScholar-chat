Photosynthesis (sample note)

Photosynthesis is the process plants use to make their own food. It happens mainly in the leaves, inside small structures called chloroplasts. Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll, the green pigment that absorbs sunlight.

During photosynthesis, a plant takes in carbon dioxide from the air through tiny holes called stomata and absorbs water from the soil through its roots. Using energy from sunlight, the plant changes carbon dioxide and water into glucose, a sugar that gives the plant energy, and releases oxygen into the air.

The word equation is: carbon dioxide + water, in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll, gives glucose + oxygen. Photosynthesis is important because it produces the oxygen we breathe and forms the base of most food chains.
